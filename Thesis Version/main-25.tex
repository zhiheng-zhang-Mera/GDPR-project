\title{Privacy Lens: An Evidence-Bounded Android Privacy Review Framework with Cryptographically Governed Legal-Evidence Packs (Revision 22)}

\begin{abstract}
\small\singlespacing
Mobile privacy-review tools face an evidence-to-decision gap: a permission observation can indicate activity but cannot by itself establish purpose, necessity, lawful basis, acquisition completeness, or legal compliance. This thesis addresses that gap through Privacy Lens, a local-first Android prototype that converts bounded permission-audit summaries into traceable prompts for human review.

Following a design-science method, the architecture treats provenance, temporal scope, missing context, and uncertainty as first-class evidence rather than converting a count into a legal verdict. A fail-closed engine rejects malformed records and unsafe temporal aggregation, isolates simulator data, and records the rule-pack version applied to each finding. Jurisdiction mappings remain outside the Android, repository, and interface layers through a replaceable pack contract; the European Union General Data Protection Regulation provides the evaluated legal objective.

Revision 22 makes the reviewed source-record digest reproducible from a canonical order-independent bundle and requires an Ed25519 signature, application-controlled trust anchor, valid and non-revoked signer, and current attestation before reassurance. The EU pack deliberately remains unattested and the production trust store empty. Findings remain local; backup and remote updates are disabled; the evaluated package has no network or sensitive runtime permission; and users can clear evidence directly. The interface separates signal, missing context, and proportionate action.

In a fixed-seed 1,000-case corpus, the engine matched its specified oracle with 800 true positives and 200 true negatives. A further 1,800 independently calculated pack cases, source and signature mutations, unknown and revoked signer probes, ordering invariance, accessibility contracts, and release-privacy contracts passed. The 1.10.0 APK and AAB built successfully. On one OPPO PERM00, the interface exposed the absent signed-review state and bounded next action; five final-artifact cold starts returned status ok with an empty crash buffer.

These results support an initial store-submission engineering candidate within the recorded scope. Signature verification authenticates only a payload relative to a configured key. The evidence does not establish reviewer identity or qualification, source-content authenticity, legal correctness, unrestricted Android visibility, accessibility conformance, participant outcomes, long-run reliability, store approval, or publication acceptance. The contribution is an evidence-bounded architecture and traceability method that separate statutory motivation, engineering heuristics, observed evidence, missing legal facts, and prohibited conclusions.
\end{abstract}
