## Panel judgement

- The interface now teaches a stable interpretation model instead of relying on users to infer how a technical signal should become an action.
- The expanded card places unknowns before action and repeats the legal boundary after the action suggestion.
- The synthetic demo discloses consequences and reversibility before execution, preserving informed choice.
- The implementation avoids fear-based urgency, legal certainty, or a request to confront developers.
- Device screenshots show the intended states are legible, but they cannot establish comprehension, reduced anxiety, calibrated trust, or adoption.

## Publication position

Revision 17 strengthens the design contribution and makes a later participant protocol testable. A Q1/Q2 publication claim still requires independent legal review, recruited participants, assistive-technology sessions, multi-device and long-duration evidence, external replication, and peer review. The score ceiling is empirical, not cosmetic.
