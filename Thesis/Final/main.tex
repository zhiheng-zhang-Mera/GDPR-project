\begin{abstract}
\small\singlespacing
Privacy Lens is a local-first Android research prototype that transforms heterogeneous, bounded privacy evidence into review prompts without treating a technical signal as proof of purpose, necessity, lawful basis, compliance, or intent. Its fail-closed pipeline preserves provenance, temporal scope, regulation-pack identity, missing context, and unavailable outputs through admission, temporal combination, typed policy interpretation, persistence, and presentation.

The contribution is an evidence-bounded architecture and evaluation method connecting executable safety properties to explicit claim limits. Versioned packs own jurisdiction-dependent mappings; a typed graph and policy output space permit review obligations but exclude legal verdicts. Deterministic, adversarial, restart, and curated mutation suites evaluate semantic conformance, while a FlowDroid adapter evaluates provenance-preserving interoperability rather than detector superiority.

The strongest external engineering evidence comprises a hash-pinned open-source census of \FDroidCensusN{} APKs, bounded physical-device workflows for \DevicePackagesN{} package identities with \CompletedWorkflowsN{} completed and \FailedDeviceWorkflowsN{} retained failures, and a one-device interface record for release \PrivacyLensVersion{}. Missing static-analysis artifacts remain missing evidence and corpus-derived counts are not converted into legal labels. These results support reproducible engineering claims only: independent legal mapping review, participant comprehension, multi-OEM replication, population accuracy, production signing, and store acceptance remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}
