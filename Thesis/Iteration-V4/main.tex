\begin{abstract}
\small\singlespacing
Privacy Lens is a local-first Android prototype converting bounded permission-audit summaries into human-review prompts. A signal cannot establish purpose, necessity, lawful basis, compliance, or intent.

The architecture makes provenance, temporal scope, missing context, and uncertainty evidence. A fail-closed engine rejects malformed records, isolates simulation, and binds findings to replaceable packs. Legal reassurance requires verified source bytes, signed monotonic governance, and two witnesses; production authority remains empty.

The system matches an order-independent multiset of data or sensor observations only when the active regulation pack supplies a valid risk-specific window, requirements, legal references, and rationale. A separate compiler validates the profile and converts it for a pure evaluator, so changing packs changes the mapping without embedding GDPR constants in detection. The result is an evidence receipt and review prompt, never blocking or a legal verdict. GDPR currently has five profiles; a research baseline has a different profile to test pack substitution.

Iteration V4 adds a minimal restart-safe temporal ledger and a Debug-only controlled device fixture compiled from the mounted profile. The ledger retains only normalised, deduplicated, same-pack observations within the largest active window; incompatible, expired, future, or malformed restart state is discarded. On 21 August 2026, an offline debug APK was installed on an OPPO PERM00 device and its profile-derived fixture added one explicitly labelled controlled-device temporal finding without a crash-buffer entry. The native \texttt{BuildConfig.DEBUG} boundary, rather than an offline bundle's JavaScript development flag, controls release exclusion. This remains synthetic engineering evidence: it verifies transport and presentation, not a real incident, legal outcome, or cross-device coverage. The contribution separates signal, legal evidence, action, and external validation.
\end{abstract}
